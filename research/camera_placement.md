# Fixed camera placement on 60–100 m multi-role vessels (naval / coast guard / offshore / research)

Research note for the "resource governor" virtual-sensor design. Compiled 2026-10-16 from public
regulator, class-society and manufacturer material. Where a claim rests only on vendor literature
or a search snippet, it is marked as such; where a standard's text could not be opened it is
marked "unconfirmed". Nothing below is a clause citation unless the clause number is stated.

## 2. Zones that usually have NO fixed coverage on a 60–100 m vessel

- **Stern ramp water approach and boat-recovery sea area** – cameras, where fitted, look at the ramp/boat deck, not at the RHIB 50 m astern; nothing in LARS vendor literature or public OPV specs confirms a camera at all [S9].
- **Mission bay / multi-mission deck on non-SOLAS naval and research ships** – SOLAS II-2/20.4.4 applies only to vehicle/ro-ro/special-category spaces; naval mission bays depend on the yard/navy CCTV scope, which is not published [S12][S15].
- **Hangar interior on ships with telescopic or no hangar** (coast guard, research, OSV) – nothing drives it.
- **Open working decks of research vessels** other than the A-frame/launch area – coverage, when present, is one camera for the bridge [S10]; winch drums get cameras more often than the deck itself [S19][S24].
- **Flight deck on offshore/research vessels outside Norway** – CAP 437 does not require a camera, so it is an owner option; expect coverage only where the HMS vendor bundled a recorder (e.g. Kongsberg AVR-100) [S1][S4].
- **Machinery spaces on older or UMS-only commercial/research hulls** – no class or SOLAS requirement; cameras appear only when the owner specified them [S16].
- **Mooring decks at this size** – usually visible from the bridge wings, so bridge-wing cameras, not mooring-deck cameras, are what you find [S20].

## 3. Implications for a virtual-sensor design

- **Assume a flight-deck camera on naval ships with embarked aircraft and on Norwegian/NCS offshore vessels; treat it as a PTZ** that may be pointed off-deck, so the "flight deck clear / people on deck" sensor must report *unknown* when the field of view does not include the deck, and must work in low light (naval feeds are often low-light/thermal).
- **Machinery-space and hangar cameras on naval/coast-guard ships are usually IPMS cameras** (PoE, ONVIF, H.264/H.265) displayed on platform-management consoles; access is via the IPMS/automation network, which is separate from any welfare/security CCTV network – plan for two video sources and two authorisation paths.
- **Stern ramp, boat deck, USV/UUV handling and research working decks need a portable camera** (or a mission-system camera) in the baseline design; fixed coverage is "sometimes" at best and is never guaranteed by any standard.
- **Mission bay occupancy on naval ships** should be derived from door/crane/winch/IPMS signals or crew confirmation; only SOLAS ro-ro/vehicle spaces (keel laid ≥ 2026) guarantee full-space, high-mounted, recorded video.
- **Deck machinery "in use"** is best read from the winch drive/IPMS; where a winch camera exists (anchor-handling, towing, research winches) use it as corroboration, not as the primary sensor.
- **Crew confirmation remains the only option** for: hangar state on ships without a permanent hangar, stern-ramp water approach, mission-bay contents on non-SOLAS ships, and any zone where the only camera is a PTZ currently slewed elsewhere.
- **Networking**: shipboard IP CCTV is wired (PoE) on managed, class-certified switches, often in a ring (vendor-claimed recovery < 20–50 ms); no class rule on CCTV-network redundancy was found, and no source described Wi-Fi/portable cameras as part of a ship's CCTV – treat a portable camera as a separate, best-effort feed [S17][S31][S32][S33].
